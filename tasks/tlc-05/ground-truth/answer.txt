HOW WELL SPECIALIZED RIDE OPTIONS APPEAR TO WORK
NYC TLC reported completed trips, April 2026

BOTTOM LINE
The high-volume FHV (HVFHV) file contains 20,995,953 completed trips with April pickup times. Shared rides were requested on 419,341 trips (2.00% of HVFHV trips), and 255,458 were marked as matched: 60.92% (60.77%-61.07%) of requests. Wheelchair-accessible-vehicle (WAV) rides were requested on 65,246 trips (0.31%), with 65,246 marked as matched: 100.00% (99.99%-100.00%).
On the supplied completed-trip indicators, shared matching was mixed and WAV matching was high. These are not fulfillment rates for all app requests: canceled requests and requests that never became completed trips are absent. The results therefore describe how recorded completed trips were labeled, not whether every New Yorker who sought either option received it.

DATA, ELIGIBILITY, AND DEFINITIONS
I analyzed the supplied April 2026 HVFHV, regular-FHV, and taxi-zone files. The primary denominator is 20,995,953 HVFHV rows with pickup timestamps from 2026-04-01 through 2026-04-30. The source had 20,995,953 rows: 0 lacked a parseable pickup time and 0 had a non-April pickup. Eligible pickup timestamps ran from 2026-04-01 00:00:00 to 2026-04-30 23:59:59.
A shared request is shared_request_flag=Y; a shared success is shared_match_flag=Y within those requests, meaning a separately booked co-rider was matched. A WAV request is wav_request_flag=Y, while wav_match_flag=Y means the trip was provided in a WAV. Thus a WAV match without a WAV request can legitimately represent a WAV serving a rider who did not request one; it is not counted in request-to-match success. Access-A-Ride (AAR) is a separate trip-volume flag, not a request-success outcome.
Match-rate confidence intervals are Wilson 95% intervals. They describe precision of the monthly proportion, not uncertainty from missing/canceled requests. Pickup geography comes from the supplied taxi-zone lookup. Wait is request-to-pickup; valid values are chronological and 0-120 minutes. Request-to-on-scene and on-scene-to-pickup use the same chronological rule (boarding capped at 30 minutes). Ride duration must be positive and no more than 240 minutes. Per-mile measures require 0.5-200 miles and a derived 1-80 mph; fare summaries require positive base passenger fare.

Operationally, 96.63% of matched shared trips and 77.42% of matched WAV-request trips had a wait inside the validity rule. Every one of the 65,246 completed trips with a WAV request was marked as provided in a WAV, but its valid median wait was 7.1 minutes (p90 12.5), 3.1 minutes above the ordinary benchmark. For context, ordinary HVFHV trips had a median request-to-pickup wait of 4.0 minutes (p90 9.2) among 20,254,895 valid waits. This broad benchmark is not matched on route, provider, rider, or time and should not be read as a causal service penalty.

LIMITATIONS
1. Completed-trip selection is the main limitation. The files omit app requests that were canceled, rejected, or never dispatched. A high match fraction among completed trips can coexist with poor population-level availability. No response-time service standard or external benchmark is supplied.
2. Flags are operator-reported service indicators. A shared_match Y does not reveal added detour, promised discount, or whether the matched passenger actually boarded. A WAV_match Y does not establish the rider's disability, vehicle condition, boarding success, or accessibility quality. AAR Y is not a request denominator.
3. Wait starts at the recorded request time and is available only for completed HVFHV trips. On-scene time has provider-dependent missingness. Fare is base passenger fare, not a standardized rider bill; the data do not identify quoted prices or discounts. Minutes/mile and fare/mile reduce but do not remove route mix, traffic, toll, airport, surge, or provider differences.
4. Data-quality exclusions are metric-specific. Among April HVFHV rows, 0 lacked request time, 0 lacked dropoff time, 268,958 had request after pickup, 0 had dropoff before pickup, 0 lacked pickup location, 0 had an unmapped location, 29 had nonpositive trip_time, 1,857 had nonpositive miles, and 25,604 had nonpositive base fare. Flag-valid rows remain in request/match denominators even when excluded from a performance metric.
5. Geography is pickup zone, not rider residence; airport/EWR and unknown zones are retained. Zone rankings require at least 100 shared or 30 WAV requests, but multiple comparisons and unmeasured composition remain. Time comparisons use local recorded pickup times.
6. This is one month (April 2026). Daily extremes are descriptive, not a trend or seasonality estimate. Yellow and green files have no comparable shared-request, WAV, or AAR fields; passenger_count was not used as a proxy because a party traveling together is not necessarily a pooled or accessible-service trip.

ASSESSMENT
Within the limited universe of recorded completed HVFHV trips, the strongest evidence is the shared request-to-match rate of 60.92% and WAV rate of 100.00%, alongside the reported wait, geographic, time, and provider differences above. These indicators can show whether specialized labels on completed rides commonly culminated in a match and whether those rides look operationally slower or more costly. They cannot establish equitable access or overall success for New Yorkers without all-request, cancellation, vehicle-supply, rider-needs, promised-versus-actual service, and satisfaction data.
